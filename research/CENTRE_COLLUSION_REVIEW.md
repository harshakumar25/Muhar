# Muhar: centre collusion and fairness checkpoints

**Research date: 9 November 2026. Status: evidence and design recommendations, not a measurement of national fraud prevalence or a deployed control.**

## What the evidence supports

An invigilator's presence cannot be the security boundary. An insider can misuse authorised access, collude with a candidate, ignore assistance, or alter a record after submission. Digitising the question paper changes the custody path but still leaves content administrators, centre networks, candidate devices, response records, and human supervision exposed. Conversely, honest invigilators have detected fraud. Muhar should design for dishonest staff without treating every centre or candidate as guilty.

The claim that some people will help candidates for money is supported by documented investigations of paid exam fraud. The specific claim that this normally costs “a few rupees,” or that a known percentage of centres collude, is **not established** by the sources below. Detected cases are a selected sample; they cannot supply a national incident rate. A [10 February 2025 Ministry of Education answer to Lok Sabha](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/184/AU993_2ENY1I.pdf?source=pqals) says the Ministry does not centrally maintain examination-specific incident data across the different conducting bodies. Its absence is a research gap, not evidence that fraud is rare or universal.

These records concern different modes and institutions. Their common lesson is a reusable set of custody and accountability requirements; they do not justify transferring an allegation from one provider or centre to another. The Supreme Court's 2024 NEET judgment also declined to infer a systemic nationwide compromise on the material then available. Muhar must preserve that distinction between a concrete local failure and the scope of a remedy.

## Threat, control, and remaining failure

The following controls are **proposed design responses**, not claims that the cited incidents would certainly have been prevented.

| Failure path | Proposed control | What still cannot be proved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staff give answers, allow communication, or ignore cheating | Separate appointment of centre staff and external observers; disclose conflicts; controlled duty allocation; independent complaint route; documented inspections and retained observation evidence | Several people can collude; a signed “no incident” report can be false. Cameras have blind spots and may fail. |
| A compromised CBT device or network receives unauthorised assistance | Reviewed device image; externally verified software release; restrict administrator and network privileges; monitor unexpected access; export security events to an independently operated collector | An approved binary can run on hostile hardware; telemetry generated by a compromised endpoint can lie or disappear. No claim of proof of pixels. |
| Candidates are steered to a favourable centre or seat | Freeze allocation policy and authorised changes; preserve signed allocation/change records; audit unusual patterns privately, with human investigation | Travel, disability accommodations, language availability, and genuine capacity constraints can explain unusual allocations. An anomaly alone is not guilt. |
| Candidate impersonation or unauthorised substitution | Institution-approved identity checks at admission and private seat/session binding; record authorised identity exceptions and resolution | Identity checks can be mistaken or bypassed. Biometric failure must have a supervised fallback and appeal; it is not an automatic fraud verdict. |
| Answers change after the candidate submits | CBT: versioned response records, explicit final submission, independently retained receipt and correction history. OMR: witnessed sealing, counted custody transfers, controlled scanning, candidate access to the recorded sheet under the exam's policy | A dishonest device can commit false input; OMR alteration before the first trusted image remains possible. A hash proves consistency with captured bytes, not original physical truth. |
| Centre staff erase evidence or suppress a complaint | Preserve records in a separate evidence custodian; test missing-record alarms; allow a secure complaint outside the local centre hierarchy; issue a durable receipt before evidence retention expires | An external archive cannot restore evidence that was never captured. False complaints still require fair investigation. |
| A local failure causes an unjustified national cancellation or collective accusation | Prepublish incident classes, decision owner, evidence standards, proportionate response options, candidate support, and appeal route | The technical system cannot determine legal culpability or substitute for institutional and judicial decisions. |

There is a hard boundary for the first paper-protection iteration: **even a correctly sealed paper can be delivered to a corrupt centre**. The first release should report separately whether the paper's custody/release checks passed and whether delivery, identity, response custody, and human-supervision evidence is complete. It must not transform a passed release check into a “fair exam” verdict.

## Checkpoints that must enter the proposal

| Checkpoint | Reviewable evidence and accountable owner | Mock rejection or escalation condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 — Centre eligibility before allocation | Operator-approved centre inventory: tested usable seats, accessibility, power/network resilience, staff conflicts, external inspection result, and prior incident review | An untested site or failed required facility does not count as deployable capacity. Centre count alone is insufficient. |
| C2 — Staff and candidate allocation | Policy version, duty roster, centre/seat assignment, every exception, and independent reviewer access; candidate mapping stays private | Unauthorised reassignment or missing approval triggers investigation. Preserve legitimate accommodation exceptions. |
| C3 — Device and package acceptance | Signed software/package versions, independent installation check, correct form/language, private device-session mapping, monitor status | Changed package, unexplained device replacement, or missing required evidence pauses the synthetic mock. |
| C4 — Release and during-exam observation | Authorised release receipt, observer status, network/security event coverage, power/link incidents, extra-time decisions, and independent complaint acknowledgements | Simulate a colluding staff pair and withheld telemetry. The report must identify the unsupported supervision claim even when the paper hash remains valid. |
| C5 — Submission and custody closure | Signed response-finalisation receipt or witnessed physical collection; transfer/scanning counts; explicit correction record | A changed response, missing sheet, or unexplained post-submit write fails the custody check and preserves evidence. |
| C6 — Challenge and decision | Candidate-readable evidence, secure channel bypassing local staff, named investigator, response/appeal deadline, redacted decision and remedy | A complaint with no durable receipt or no accountable response fails the governance test. |
| C7 — Final audit cutoff | Witnessed checkpoint and complete scan after cases and appeals close, referencing redacted outcomes and private custody references | A proof bundle from exam day cannot certify later grievance history. Missing later evidence remains `INCOMPLETE`. |

For each checkpoint, distinguish an **enforced condition**, a **human attestation**, an **independent observation**, and **missing evidence**. The first three are not interchangeable. A centre manager signing their own checklist supplies an attestation, not independent verification.

## Generalise the evidence interface, then validate each delivery mode

| Exam mode | Common Muhar objects | Mode-specific assurance still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CBT admission/recruitment tests | Session charter, content commitment, release authorisation, centre receipt, private incident case, later public proof | Endpoint integrity, network supervision, identity checks, response finalisation, accessibility, and validated form comparability across shifts |
| OMR/paper tests | Same charter and commitments; custody-transfer and packet-opening records | Printing, transport, storage, physical opening, booklet/OMR matching, collection and scanning. Late digital release cannot protect paper already printed days earlier. |
| Descriptive written examinations | Same authorised-paper and custody record; marking-policy version | Answer-book custody, assessor assignment, moderation and re-evaluation. A paper hash does not establish fair grading. |
| Skill/practical/oral examinations | Session/rubric commitment, appointment/conflict record, evidence-preservation and appeal objects | Task equivalence, assessor conduct, recording consent/privacy, scoring reliability. These are a separate assessment project, not a trivial CBT adapter. |

Start the protocol core with one synthetic CBT integration and test the schema against a paper-custody walkthrough. Describe the other modes as compatibility targets until their controls are validated. Keep scoring, psychometric validity, disability accommodations, and institutional remedy authority explicit. Technology that preserves a biased paper or an inaccessible examination faithfully has not established fair opportunity or candidate quality.

## Validation and uncertainty ledger

- **Verified source status:** official court, Parliament, ED, and PIB records above were inspected; criminal allegations, cognizance, and conviction are labelled separately.
- **Unknown:** national share of corrupt centres, staff bribery distribution, undetected incident rate, comparative causal effect of CBT versus paper, and effectiveness of Muhar controls.
- **Not run:** centre simulation, telemetry-loss drill, impersonation/fallback drill, response-tampering fixture, candidate interview, or live exam. These remain proposed tests.
- **Required first discriminating drill:** let a synthetic centre receive the correct signed paper, then simulate prohibited assistance or altered responses. The report must preserve a passed paper-custody claim while failing or withholding the affected supervision/response claim; it must never issue an overall fairness certificate.
