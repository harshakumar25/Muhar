# Muhar: governance-first build plan

Status: design proposal, updated 9 November 2026. The first technical scope is a synthetic sealed-paper pipeline followed by a low-stakes, one-centre CBT mock. The reusable protocol targets entrance, recruitment, eligibility, and university exams through explicit profiles. [PROPOSAL.md](PROPOSAL.md) sets the current five real-world acceptance gates; [SEALED_PAPER_ARCHITECTURE.md](SEALED_PAPER_ARCHITECTURE.md) specifies the confidentiality experiment. [Iteration 2](research/ITERATION_2.md) remains the evidence-log baseline. No live-exam use is authorized by these documents.

## 1. The idea and the honest promise

India's exam-leak problem is a chain of custody and coordination problem. Question authors, bank curators, exam operators, software vendors, centre staff, printers, and investigators all hold different pieces of power. Muhar makes the *rules, draw, release decision, and later corrections* checkable across these boundaries. The legally responsible exam operator still runs the exam and makes incident decisions.

**Promise for v0:** after a mock exam, anyone with the complete named log and retained checkpoints can check that its inspected history contains one authorized freeze for the official session, that the manifest and rules were independently observed before a chosen future randomness round under stated timing assumptions, and that the disclosed paper follows the draw. Later witnessed checkpoints expose recorded exceptions through the declared audit cutoff. **Limits:** this does not prove the bank was academically sound, that a human did not leak an item, that a centre was physically secure, that no document existed outside the named log, or that a signed statement about a real-world event was truthful.

**Additional first-iteration target:** test compartmented item access, encrypted storage, protected assembly, and independently approved release with synthetic questions. A single routine operational role should not obtain the assembled plaintext before its declared release under the named hardware/key-policy assumptions. A simulator does not establish host-administrator resistance; actual attested hardware and key-policy tests are a separate required gate. Authors see their items, and endpoints see released content. Measure those exposures instead of promising universal invisibility.

This answers the Devcon theme through four lenses:

| Lens | Muhar's concrete contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Governance | A published charter defines who may freeze, approve, release, cancel, audit, and investigate an exam. Every exceptional action has an accountable signer and reason. |
| Real world | Start with a digital mock because printing and transport add separate custody risks. Record centre checks and incidents as evidence, subject to human investigation. |
| Coordination | Operator, bank auditor, independent witnesses, and later release custodians agree on one version of the rules and one visible event history. |
| Decentralisation | Distribute only powers that benefit from it: the key shares, monitoring, and independent verification. Keep legal responsibility with the exam operator. |

Ethereum's **candidate** contribution is an independent public anchor for the signed pre-seed manifest and log checkpoint. The operational baseline is a small, complete transparency log. Standard witnesses check checkpoint consistency; an independent content monitor scans all leaves, and a public verifier can repeat that scan. The mock compares this baseline with a research-only shadow anchor over the same frozen record, including a case where witness archives disappear. A chain stores a commitment; it does **not** verify an off-chain incident or keep the underlying log available. Keep the anchor optional unless that comparison shows a meaningful gain. [C2SP's witness protocol](https://c2sp.org/tlog-witness@main) and [Ethereum's proof-of-stake documentation](https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/) describe the respective mechanisms and limits.

## 2. Why now, and what institutions actually need

The [October 2026 parliamentary committee release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2321420&lang=1&reg=1) calls for larger question banks, papers set close to the exam, independent security auditing, secure grievances, and attention to the new risks of computer-based testing. These are reasons to test a narrow evidence protocol, not endorsements of Muhar or of a particular blockchain deployment.

An [NTA-hosted draft CBT tender](https://nta.ac.in/Download/Tender/Tender_20260302181817.pdf) describes providing a bilingual question-paper soft copy one hour before the exam start. That is a concrete procurement design constraint to investigate, not a universal rule for Indian exams or an adopted Muhar schedule. A pilot should simulate both this `T−60m` handoff and a near-start handoff, measure how long selected content is exposed, and have a willing operator choose its actual policy before a seed is known. The draw must precede whichever handoff the charter selects. A late public proof is useful for retrospective audit but may arrive after a candidate's remedy window; the charter needs timely procedural-integrity review.

The [Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20100/1/a2024-01.pdf) expressly covers leaks, unauthorized access, and tampering for the public examinations within its scope; a [2026 amendment received presidential assent](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298901&lang=2&reg=48). [DoPT's summary of the 2024 Rules](https://persmin.gov.in/DOPT_achievements_2014-2025/InnerFiles/Current_Year_PDF/2025.pdf) describes the official incident-reporting and monitoring mechanisms. Muhar must export evidence into those workflows; it cannot decide legal guilt or replace reporting. A state, university, or private adopter must map its own obligations separately.

## 3. Actors and decision rights

The [capacity ledger](research/INDIA_CAPACITY_EVIDENCE.md) separates annual unique people, repeated sittings, and simultaneous usable seats. Do not size a deployment from centre counts or assume NEET is twice JEE. The [centre review](research/CENTRE_COLLUSION_REVIEW.md) makes staff collusion, impersonation, and post-submit response changes independent failure paths. A correct paper does not certify fair supervision.

| Actor | What they control | What checks them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exam operator | Exam scope, schedule, syllabus, final go/no-go, centre operations, incident response | Signed charter and manifest, external audit, public record, applicable law |
| Bank authors and reviewers | Question content and academic metadata | Compartmentalized access, conflict declarations, duplicate review, audit trail |
| Independent bank audit function | Reviews approval coverage, conflicts, duplicate/quality process and assigned samples; specialist reviewers see bounded item sets | Signed scope and cumulative access map; a whole-bank plaintext export defeats the stronger sealed-paper premise |
| Checkpoint witnesses | Independently retain and cosign consistent log checkpoints; the mock requires both named witnesses on the same checkpoint | Public cosignatures, checkpoint exchange, and a declared quorum; signatures alone do not inspect leaves |
| Independent content monitor | Downloads all leaves through each required checkpoint, checks signatures and duplicate freezes, and signs a root-and-size-bound report | Public full log export and an independent verifier repeating the scan |
| Release custodians (first synthetic prototype) | Two-of-three signed release approvals under a fixed policy; no plaintext preview | Separately operated roles, recipient/session/time binding, no unilateral policy bypass; approval quorum is not cryptographic threshold decryption |
| Assembly/key-policy administrators | Approved software identity, attested key-delivery rules, deployment and recovery configuration | Independent change approval and bypass tests; a single root principal that can override these controls remains a declared trusted actor |
| Centre officials | Device and local custody checks, receipt of release, incident reports | Dual sign-off, external checks, official reporting chain |
| Candidates and public | Verify post-exam evidence and submit concerns | No candidate wallet or public personal-data trail required |
| Muhar maintainers | Public code, protocol, releases | They hold no exam secrets and cannot override an exam decision |

Before a real pilot, the operator and independent participants must sign a short charter specifying the quorum, conflicts of interest, substitution, emergency authority, evidence-retention period, publication schedule, permitted late admission, candidate challenge window, response owner, and who can cancel or postpone an exam. The institution must authenticate the authorized signing root, named public log, and witness keyset. For the one-session mock, **both named independent witnesses cosign the same checkpoint** before the seed; a missing signature pauses the session. Their standard signatures establish consistency of log history, not uniqueness of a freeze. A separate independent monitor downloads and scans every leaf through that checkpoint, binds its report to the checkpoint root and size, and watches for new leaves through seed, handoff, and start. A fresh signed monitor status is required at handoff and before start; a missed deadline pauses the mock. This is a **provisional start gate**. The public verifier repeats the full scan through later witnessed checkpoints, including one after all candidate appeals close, before reporting a final one-freeze result through the declared cutoff. Within the inspected named log prefix, exactly one authorized freeze for the official session is required. Missing leaves yield `INCOMPLETE`; duplicate freezes yield `FAILED`. These claims depend on contemporaneously retained checkpoint evidence, archive availability, and an honest independent observer; they cannot prove the absence of documents advertised outside the named system. An authenticated per-session map is deferred until scale warrants verified log-to-map transitions. The Ethereum experiment additionally pins a chain and immutable registry. A self-signed manifest cannot establish official authority. A lost keyholder or missed beacon round must have a pre-agreed response; it must not create an invisible override. **After the seed is known, cancellation could be used to retry until a preferred paper appears.** Require an independent quorum, logged incident evidence, a published reason and after-action review; link every replacement to the cancelled session and prohibit silent reuse of the same bank under a new round. The verifier must show the full chain of attempts. No parallel precommitted alternatives may be chosen after the seed.

## 4. End-to-end design

```text
private bank + reviewed metadata       signed frozen manifest
                |                               |
         salted commitments  ------> public transparency log
                |                               |
                |                      experimental Ethereum anchor
                |                               |
          future drand round  ------------------+
                |                               |
       deterministic paper draw          monitors copy/check history
                |                               |
         exam-time release                      |
                +------> post-exam proof bundle + independent verifier
```

### 4.1 Prepare and freeze

The operator approves a blueprint with explicit quotas (for example, subject and difficulty) and a reviewed eligible inventory. A canonical, versioned manifest binds the publicly announced exam/session identifier, **one ordered inventory root**, blueprint commitment, selection algorithm version, beacon network identity and **specific future round**, admission/release policy, correction/cancellation policy, and authorized signer keys. Each inventory record contains one opaque item ID, one selection stratum, a salted question-package commitment, and a separately salted answer-key commitment. Each content commitment binds the protocol version, session, item ID, salt, and canonical content. The question package covers every language variant, media asset, option order, and rendering rule; its commitment opens independently of the answer key. A bare hash of a guessable question can reveal it by dictionary search, so content commitments need independent random salts. The independent bank auditor signs an attestation to this **exact manifest before the beacon round**. The manifest and attestation enter a complete, witnessed log with a conservative time buffer before that round. The independent monitor scans all leaves and reports the authorized freeze count against the signed checkpoint root and size. In the Ethereum experiment, **one** transaction anchors a digest binding protocol version, official session ID, manifest hash, and pre-seed witnessed checkpoint hash. To claim it was finalized before the round, the experiment must preserve verifiable evidence of pre-round finality; a pre-round block later finalized does not establish that claim. Otherwise its timing result is `INCOMPLETE`, while a demonstrated late anchor is `FAILED`. The mock charter declares the witnessed-log profile; the shadow anchor cannot authorize a fallback. For the synthetic mock, propose a 24-hour manifest freeze buffer and test the selected-form handoff separately; tune timings only after operational tests. [Ethereum's proof-of-stake documentation](https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/) distinguishes transaction inclusion from finality.

The operator can append a signed amendment before freeze. After freeze, the only permitted path is an explicit signed cancellation/reschedule and a new session identifier, subject to the charter's stricter post-seed quorum. A replacement must never silently inherit the old evidence. The monitor and public verifier count all authorized freeze events for the official session in the named log through the declared audit checkpoint; a second event fails the one-freeze claim even if a one-time Ethereum registry still holds only the first hash. Both analyses need the complete off-chain bundle. The one shadow anchor covers its pre-seed prefix only, so later cancellation and grievance history depends on later witnessed checkpoints and mirrored leaves. No question text, answer key, candidate identifier, leaked image, secret, or grievance narrative goes on-chain. [Ethereum's privacy guidance](https://ethereum.org/privacy/ethereum) explains why public-chain data should be treated as visible and persistent.

### 4.2 Draw

After the committed future round, `muhar-draw` verifies the beacon signature and derives a deterministic selection from the committed, ordered eligibility metadata. The first protocol supports fixed quotas across disjoint strata: each item belongs to exactly one stratum. The algorithm must specify canonical serialization, domain separation, random-byte derivation, unbiased integer sampling, quotas, tie-breaking, and failure behavior when a quota is impossible. Use established cryptographic libraries; do not invent a beacon or cryptographic primitive. The protocol and test vectors must let an independent implementation reproduce identical IDs. [drand's specification](https://docs.drand.love/docs/specification/) describes verifiable randomness rounds.

**Important secrecy limit:** anyone who can read the full bank can learn the selected paper as soon as the public seed arrives. The mock charter must fix admission and late-entry rules before the round. It compares a `T−60m` paper handoff with a near-start handoff; the seed must precede either handoff, so the earlier option lengthens the exposure window. A real operator may permit late entry; that choice, a separate late-entry form if used, and its fairness review must be decided before freezing the manifest. This still leaves insider and in-room communication risks. For multiple shifts or accommodations, design separate session/release policies and protect reusable items. A paper requiring off-site printing days beforehand cannot gain a just-in-time property without redesigning the print process. The old tagline “Sealed until the gates close” is therefore a poor security promise; a session's actual admission policy controls the risk.

### 4.3 Release and record

The first technical milestone exercises the **complete sealed-paper path with synthetic items**: scoped review and client encryption, versioned ciphertext storage, deterministic assembly in an approved protected worker, recipient-bound encrypted delivery, and signed release permits. Compare an ordinary isolated service with an attested worker on appropriate hardware; the former exposes plaintext to a privileged host administrator and cannot substantiate the stronger confidentiality claim. Test two-of-three signed approvals as an application quorum, and audit every key-policy, root, backup, and recovery path for unilateral bypass. The [sealed architecture](SEALED_PAPER_ARCHITECTURE.md) defines the exact visibility and trust model. Live questions remain out of scope until independent review.

At authorised release, the approved signing process binds the digest of a package containing exactly the selected, opened questions in draw order. The centre verifies and signs what it received; the CBT import/start path rejects unsigned or mismatched packages and records the digest. Participants receive a readable full digest and signed printed exit receipt, with QR optional. Keep the bare digest private until the last affected session and bind it publicly with a salted commitment. These records are delivery attestations; a colluding centre can still assist candidates or lie about display. Preserve independent supervision and response-submission evidence as separate claims. Admission-gate closure is an attested physical fact. A public beacon or timelock alone does not authorize release. A full-bank insider can reconstruct the form once selection inputs are known; key custody cannot erase that knowledge. Signed centre builds need an external trusted install/boot check and inspection, rather than self-hashing.

`muhar-log` stores signed public events and encrypted/restricted private evidence separately. Public events include commitments, approvals, cancellations, beacon references, salted commitments to release digests, and later audit outcomes. Bare paper digests and detailed centre receipts are disclosed only after the last affected session. A reviewed append-only log provides inclusion and consistency proofs. The v0 monitor exports and scans **every leaf** through the signed checkpoint, checks the official session ID and authorized signatures, counts freezes, and watches new leaves through seed and start; the public verifier repeats complete scans against later checkpoints. Two named standard witnesses cosign the same checkpoint for the mock and retain a post-exam and final post-appeal checkpoint. Their consistency signatures do not certify the content. The mock's experimental arm anchors **one pre-seed checkpoint per session** on Ethereum; later log checkpoints are witnessed but not additionally anchored. An anchor alone does not provide durable access to the full log, so at least two independent mirrors must preserve proof bundles.

### 4.4 Verify and disclose

For the synthetic mock, publish the full bank, ordered inventory, salts, paper, signed release/centre receipts, beacon signature, manifest, signatures, **complete log leaves through the declared audit checkpoint**, signed checkpoints and monitor report, and any experimental chain reference after the exam but **before the procedural challenge deadline**. `muhar verify` must recompute the log root and freeze count, the inventory root and exact selection, verify that the actual released paper contains those opened items in order with matching translations/assets/options, and compare its digest to delivery receipts. It reports **separate claims** for draw reproduction, paper-to-draw match, confidential bank audit, log consistency, and delivery attestation. A signed centre statement is labelled an attestation, never proof that a candidate saw the content. An absent proof is `INCOMPLETE`, never success.

For a reusable confidential bank, the public can replay the draw if the ordered inventory records and selected question-package openings are disclosed after all sessions that reuse those items. Unselected content and salts stay private. Answer-key openings follow their own approved challenge/scoring timetable. An independent auditor must privately review whether the undisclosed items and tags were valid; the Merkle root cannot prove that. If even the eligibility metadata cannot be disclosed, the claim must be narrower: **auditor-verifiable selection**, with a public audit attestation. If disclosure comes after the institutional remedy deadline, a genuinely independent reviewer needs restricted evidence **before** that deadline and must issue a reviewable timely finding. A later public proof only supports retrospective accountability. The charter must establish a procedural-integrity case type rather than assume an existing answer-key challenge covers it. Zero-knowledge proofs are a research option only after a simpler audited protocol works. The validity and fairness of the bank remain human/psychometric questions.

## 5. Minimum technical modules

| Module | First release | Later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `muhar-draw` | Go CLI, versioned manifest format, deterministic draw, known-answer vectors | Multiple forms, reviewed psychometric constraints |
| `muhar-log` | Reuse a reviewed transparency-log implementation; full-leaf export, two named checkpoint witnesses, separate full-scan monitor, and one experimental pre-seed Ethereum anchor | Higher-volume operation, verified log-backed map transitions, retention controls |
| `muhar verify` | Separate Go verifier plus one independent reference implementation and a readable proof report | Accessible web verifier with English/Hindi explanations |
| `muhar-bank` | Synthetic versioned items, scoped author/reviewer access, cumulative visibility record, encrypted question and separate answer stores | Institution-specific authoring, assessed review quality and bank-history controls |
| `muhar-seal` | Complete synthetic sealed workflow, signed application quorum and recipient/time/session binding; ordinary-host reference plus attested-hardware experiment | Independently audited deployment; threshold cryptography only if justified and implemented |
| `muhar-trace` / `muhar-radar` | Defer | Investigative canaries and leak monitoring with human review; never automatic guilt decisions |

An initial repository layout after the spec review could be `cmd/muhar/`, `internal/draw/`, `internal/verify/`, `protocol/`, `testvectors/`, `demo/`, and `docs/`. Keep the number of dependencies small. The experimental comparison uses one pre-seed anchor per session, binding the manifest and witnessed checkpoint; do not deploy a token, candidate wallet flow, or custom chain.

## 6. Open-source security and privacy

- License code under **Apache-2.0** after a contributor/IP review; publish the protocol specification, threat model, test vectors, and independent verifier. Keep only genuine exam secrets and operational credentials private. The source being public is compatible with security when the secret is in the keys and controlled processes.
- Protect `draw`, `verify`, log integration, crypto, build scripts, and release workflow with at least two independent reviewers, mandatory checks, and protected branches. A `CODEOWNERS` entry alone can be satisfied by one owner, so configure the required review count separately. [GitHub's branch-protection documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches) describes these controls.
- Pin dependencies and publish a software bill of materials, build provenance, signed release artifacts, and an independently checkable build path. Verify provenance and signature **outside** the program being checked. [SLSA](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.0/levels) and [Sigstore](https://docs.sigstore.dev/cosign/verifying/verify/) provide existing models.
- Use synthetic data in the public repository and CI. Add secret scanning, parser fuzzing, deterministic protocol vectors, dependency review, and a private vulnerability-reporting channel before public deployment. Do not publish exploitable live-exam details in issues.
- Keep candidate identities, responses, biometrics, and raw grievances out of the public log and chain. Separate purpose-limited private evidence with encryption, access records, deletion/retention rules, and incident response. Design for the phased [DPDP Act commencement](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf) and [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); a covered operator must map the applicable duties. [CERT-In's directions](https://cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf) are relevant to incident and log processes for covered entities.
- A public verification page should meet applicable [GIGW 3.0 accessibility guidance](https://guidelines.india.gov.in/new-features-of-gigw-3-0/) and work on low-bandwidth/mobile connections. A paper or offline receipt can carry an exam reference without exposing a candidate.

## 7. Milestones and proof of success

| Gate | Deliverable | Exit test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Governance and spec | Willing operator and candidate representatives review the [pilot charter draft](research/PILOT_CHARTER_DRAFT.md); sign the session, timing, evidence-disclosure, procedural challenge, response, and remedy rules before any mock | A candidate can submit a received proof or complaint in time for an accountable response; independent reviewer can explain each trust assumption and cancellation path |
| 1. Sealed synthetic pipeline and reproducible draw | Scoped encrypted bank, ordinary-host reference, attested assembly experiment, release permits, independent draw verifier | Wrong worker/session/recipient/time, missing approval, replay and key-policy bypass refuse release; host-isolation claim requires actual hardware tests; two implementations select identical IDs |
| 2. Public evidence | Small complete signed log, 2-of-2 checkpoint witnesses, active full-scan monitor, pre-seed/post-exam/post-appeal checkpoints, mirrored proofs; research-only shadow testnet anchor | A second authorized freeze after the first checkpoint fails the final claim even when witnesses cosign a consistent log; a missing leaf is `INCOMPLETE`; a late or unproven pre-round finality claim does not pass |
| 3. Mock exam and generality | Willing partner, one-centre CBT integration, dishonest-centre/response-tampering drills; replay a second synthetic exam profile; usable proof before challenge deadline | Separate paper, supervision and response-custody claims; cross-profile/session misuse fails; participants can submit cases; no live questions or personal secrets enter public systems |
| 4. Adversarial review | External review of protocol, code, key process and centre design; outage/compromise drill | Findings are fixed and retested; written go/no-go for any live-secret pilot |
| 5. Limited live-secret pilot | Institution-approved low-stakes exam with reviewed release controls | Documented release, incident, accessibility and rollback results; no high-stakes claim |

Measure the percent of independent verifiers that reproduce the paper, time from exam end to actionable proof availability, candidate case receipt and response time, missing/mismatched log events, selected-form exposure time under both handoff schedules, release lateness, outage recovery, and whether an auditor can reconstruct every exception. “Zero leaks” alone is not a meaningful pilot metric because it cannot be proved from a small test.

## 8. Decisions to settle before implementation

1. Pick the first partner and exact format: one digital mock session is the default. Multi-shift and printed exams need different release/custody designs.
2. Decide when a real operator may disclose the eligibility metadata and selected item openings. If it cannot, state the narrower auditor-verifiable claim.
3. Choose independent witnesses and bank auditor, and define how they are appointed, replaced, and funded without losing independence.
4. Specify a lawful retention and evidence-export schedule with the adopting institution; do not put sensitive evidence on an immutable public network.
5. Choose the exact beacon network/round. For the shadow Ethereum comparison, also choose and pin a testnet network/registry in the signed charter; the demo anchor is not a production security guarantee or an operational fallback.
6. Fix a proof-disclosure and remedy schedule that gives candidates usable evidence before their procedural-integrity deadline. If public disclosure must wait for bank reuse, assign an independent confidential reviewer with a timely decision and appeal path.
7. Set the actual selected-paper handoff schedule before the seed, based on the adopting CBT interface. Compare `T−60m` and near-start variants in the synthetic study; do not assume either works in a live exam.
